⚡ Brutal Design Truth
The current dark purple gradient is differentiated, but it reads "ambient wellness startup" — the same aesthetic as a sleep tracker or meditation app. FiTi is selling population health infrastructure to CMOs. Their other software looks like Epic, Salesforce, and Tableau. The new brand needs to feel like a premium B2B data platform that genuinely cares about humans — not a consumer app with a B2B pitch deck stapled on. Coral as the primary brand color breaks healthcare convention deliberately: it signals warmth without sacrificing authority.

⚡ Critical Architecture Note on "Passive Sensing"
The passive sensing described on the current website — raw location access, call log density, communication patterns — is Phase 3 technology requiring deep OS integration that iOS and Android actively restrict. For Phase 1, behavioral signals should come from Apple HealthKit and Google Fit (step count, movement patterns, sleep regularity) combined with in-app engagement data. HealthKit and Google Fit are privacy-compliant, already trusted by users, and give you 80% of the clinical signal with 5% of the engineering complexity. Build that first. Passive sensing is a moat you earn once you have clinical evidence — not the foundation you start with.

04 — Onboarding Flows

First impressions
make or break pilots.

Each role has a completely different first-run experience. The patient flow is the most critical — it must feel like joining a community, never like entering a mental health program. Scroll each flow horizontally.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑
Member Onboarding (FiTi Connect)
Most critical · Must not feel clinical · Designed for activation, not compliance
Step 01
Warm Invitation
"Dr. Kamau recommended the FiTi Community Program for you." Doctor's name is shown. Never mentions mental health or treatment.
Non-stigmatizing
Step 02
What Brings You Here
5 tappable cards. No clinical framing. "I want more social connection," "I've been feeling a bit isolated," "My doctor suggested it," etc.
No clinical framing
Step 03
Interest Survey
Visual illustrated grid. Hiking, Coffee, Books, Cooking, Music, Volunteering, Markets, Fitness… Tap to select. No wrong answers.
High engagement
Step 04
Availability
Simple calendar-style grid. Weekday mornings, evenings, weekend slots. One tap per block. Designed for people with irregular schedules.
Step 05
Privacy Setup
Explicit opt-in for each signal type. Toggle with plain English. "Allow movement data from Apple Health? This helps us understand your activity." HIPAA consent here.
Legal consent gate
Step 06
The Magic Moment ✨
Animated proximity rings expand outward. Names appear. "You've been matched with 9 people in your area who share your interests." This is the product's defining moment.
Signature moment
Step 07
First Event RSVP
Immediately shown an upcoming event for their cohort. One-tap RSVP. Creates behavioral commitment before the user leaves onboarding. Critical for retention.
Activation lock-in
Step 08
Meet the Community
Async intro wall. Each member has an avatar + 3 interest tags. No pressure to message. Group intro thread already started by facilitator.
🩺
Clinician Onboarding (FiTi Clinical)
Must take <10 minutes · EHR-first · Minimum behavior change required
Step 01
SSO Login
Login via hospital SSO or email. No new password required. One click from EHR smart button if Epic/Cerner integration is live.
Zero new friction
Step 02
Patient Import
CSV upload or FHIR sync. System pre-populates patient list. Clinician reviews and confirms. Takes 2 minutes max.
Step 03
5-Min Training
Async video walkthrough: how to read isolation scores, how to e-prescribe FiTi, when to escalate. Completable on mobile during commute.
Step 04
First Prescription
Select a patient with high isolation score. Click "Prescribe FiTi." Customize optional message. Patient receives invitation. This is the first value moment.
First value moment
Step 05
Alert Config
Set thresholds: "Alert me if isolation score spikes by X% or PHQ-9 worsens by Y points." Choose delivery: email or push notification.
📊
Admin / Health System (FiTi Command)
Formal setup process · Legal before everything · IT will be a bottleneck — plan for it
Step 01
BAA Execution
Digital signature via DocuSign. HIPAA Business Associate Agreement legally required before any PHI is shared. Cannot be skipped or delayed.
Legal gate
Step 02
Org Setup
Name, logo, location(s), clinical contact. White-label branding for patient app if purchased on Health System tier.
Step 03
Integration Config
Connect EHR FHIR endpoint. Guided wizard. Technical lead from IT required. Usually 1–3 days. This is almost always the bottleneck — surface it early.
IT bottleneck
Step 04
Population Criteria
Define pilot population by age range, ICD-10 codes (F32, F41, Z60.x), risk scores. FiTi suggests evidence-based defaults to reduce decision fatigue.
Step 05
Protocol Selection
Choose intervention templates: community type (structured/organic), event frequency, facilitator model, outcome measurement cadence.
Step 06
Launch Cohort
Upload enrollment list. System forms initial cohorts. Admin reviews matching quality and approves. Invitations sent to patients. Program is live.
First patients live

06 — Market Positioning

What FiTi is, what it isn't,
and how to say it in a boardroom.

B2B healthcare buying decisions happen in boardrooms, not app stores. The language needs to match where decisions get made. The current site tries to be everything — and ends up being specific to no one.

Tagline Options — Ranked
"Operationalizing Social Health at Scale."
Current tagline · Too jargony · Who outside healthcare ops uses "operationalizing"?
"Turn community into clinical evidence."
Recommended · Bridges clinical and human · Speaks to payers, systems, and media
↑ Use this
"Social prescribing, at scale, with proof."
Strong for healthcare system audiences who know the term "social prescribing"
↑ Or this
"The clinical infrastructure for social health."
Best for IT, procurement, and technical audiences during sales cycles
Competitive Positioning
BetterHelp / Headspace
Consumer therapy and mindfulness. No B2B clinical validation, no community formation, no outcomes reporting for payers.
Not FiTi
Epic EHR modules
Lives inside the hospital. FiTi takes patients outside into real-world community. EHR cannot do that.
Complementary
Wellframe / Carebook
Digital care management — good at adherence, not designed for community formation or social isolation measurement.
Different lane
Meetup / Facebook Groups
No clinical measurement, no HIPAA, no outcomes reporting. Consumer, not clinical infrastructure.
Not comparable
No solution (inaction)
Every lonely patient costs $1,600+/yr in excess spend. This is FiTi's real competition — status quo inertia.
Primary win case
🇰🇪 Ideal First Customer — Kenya
A Nairobi-based private hospital or employer-sponsored health plan (AAR, Jubilee, Resolution Health) with a population health mandate willing to run a 6-month pilot with 100–300 patients. Decision-maker: Medical Director or Head of Wellness. Value proposition: lower mental health claims, better member satisfaction, differentiated benefit. Clinical evidence from this pilot becomes the global pitch deck and the peer-reviewed paper.
🇺🇸 Ideal First Customer — US
A mid-size regional health system or Accountable Care Organization under value-based care contracts, with an existing behavioral health program looking to add a social health module. Buyer: CMO or VP Population Health. IRB pilot data from Kenya accelerates the US conversation from "interesting concept" to "let's see your outcomes data." SOC2 Type II certification is a pre-requisite for most US health system procurement.
07 — Build Roadmap

What to actually build.
In what order.

The current site implies a complete platform. What's needed next is the minimum product to run one real pilot with one real organization and collect real PHQ-9 outcomes. Everything else is secondary to that.

Phase 1
Prove It
Months 1–5 · Kenya pilot · Real clinical data
●Patient iOS app — interest survey, match reveal, event RSVP, group chat, PHQ-9 check-in (biweekly)
●Apple HealthKit signal integration (movement, steps — NOT raw OS sensing)
●Admin web portal — enrollment, cohort management, basic outcome tracking, CSV export
●Clinician view (basic) — patient list with risk scores, manual FiTi invitation send
●IRB protocol submitted with Nairobi Hospital or AAR Health
●30–100 enrolled patients, 8-week program, real PHQ-9 and UCLA-3 outcomes
●Replace all simulated testimonials and fake live stats on website with real data
Phase 2
Scale It
Months 6–12 · 2nd customer · Raise seed round
●Android app + WhatsApp-based MVP for lower-bandwidth Kenya users (critical for equity)
●FiTi Clinical full portal — EHR-linked, alert system, clinical e-prescription workflow
●FHIR R4 API development for Epic and Cerner integration
●Cohort matching algorithm v2 — ML-based, improving on manual v1
●White-label patient app for first paying health system customer
●SOC2 Type II certification process begins (~$75K, 9 months)
●Seed pitch deck anchored by Kenya pilot outcome data + 2nd customer LOI
Phase 3
Defend It
Months 12–24 · US/UK entry · Publication
●Passive behavioral sensing via HealthKit (not raw OS access) — isolation risk model v3
●FiTi Command full payer analytics — ROI reporting, HEDIS tracking, claims integration
●Claims data integration with first US payer or employer partner
●FDA SaMD regulatory review + legal opinion on any diagnostic claims language
●Peer-reviewed publication of Kenya pilot outcomes (this is the US market key)
●First US health system contract signed (not pilot — contract)
●Proprietary matching algorithm becomes defensible moat vs. competitors
⚡ The Single Most Important Sentence
The FiTi website is the least valuable thing you can work on right now. The most valuable thing you can do in the next 90 days is get one real organization in Nairobi to sign a pilot agreement, enroll 30 real people, and collect 8 weeks of real PHQ-9 data. That single outcome is worth more than 12 months of design work, pitch deck revisions, and website polish combined. Build the minimum product to run that pilot — nothing more — and start there. The website becomes credible the moment you have real outcomes to put on it.
